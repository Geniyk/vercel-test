1. Test the paywall → payment initiation step

Evidence: 18,706 sessions saw the paywall and 5,693 initiated payment, a 30.4% initiation rate.

Potential value: A 5 percentage-point lift would create about 935 additional payment initiations. At the observed 71.0% initiation-to-success rate and ₹314.96 observed net revenue per successful payer, that is approximately ₹2.09 lakh incremental net revenue.

Condition: Validate with an A/B test and confirm payment-success rate does not deteriorate.

Would not do: Scale acquisition spend solely on this data before testing the conversion step.

2. Run channel-specific onboarding tests

Evidence: Web and WhatsApp show different volumes and payment conversion in the observed data.

Potential value: Size only after an experiment; observational channel differences do not establish causality.

Condition: Randomized within-channel tests should improve downstream payment conversion.

Would not do: Declare one channel inherently better from these observational results.

3. Investigate repeat usage before scaling retention spend

Evidence: The weekly cohort table shows how quickly chat activity falls after acquisition.

Potential value: The supplied data does not establish the causal value of a retention intervention.

Condition: A product experiment must produce measurable incremental repeat sessions.

Would not do: Attribute retention differences to campaigns without an experiment.

Assumption: ₹314.96 is observed net revenue per successful payer (₹1,219,843 ÷ 3,873 unique successful gateway transactions) and is used only for illustrative sizing.
